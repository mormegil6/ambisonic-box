# Guest test endpoint: full behaviour

The pitch, the connection settings and the four rules that matter in practice are in the [main README](../README.md#guest-test-endpoint-the-guest-application). This page is the complete session behaviour, plus the separate owner SRT route that shares the same gateway code.

Anyone with an ambisonic microphone rig and OBS ([stock](https://obsproject.com/) over SRT, or [Music Edition](https://github.com/pkviet/obs-studio/releases/) over RTMP) can test their stream against this stack without standing up their own server: a keyless application that borrows the whole pipeline for the duration of a session.

**Disabled by default.** Most deployments are a single private publisher and should never expose a keyless application; set `GUEST_ENABLED=1` to opt in. Off, the `guest` application does not exist in the ingest config and the status pages carry no trace of it. `SRT_ENABLED=1` (the default) binds UDP 8890 but admits nobody on its own: the arbiter still refuses every caller until `GUEST_ENABLED=1`.

> **REACHABLE on the public demo at stream.bmroz.eu since 2026-08-20.** The inbound port landed: the department forwards UDP 8890 to the box, and a guest session has been carried end to end from a machine outside the faculty network - admitted by the arbiter, direct-to-DASH, sixteen channels of third-order ambisonics at 17.4 Mbps, demo loop standing down for the handover and the reconnect grace arming on disconnect. Two caveats that belong here rather than in a footnote. The forwarding rules are **provisional** until the administrator makes them permanent, so the public endpoint can vanish on a restart of equipment nobody here controls. And the forward **rewrites source addresses**, so every external caller arrives as one and the same address. Read the ban and cooldown rules below with that in mind: both are address-keyed, so on this deployment they currently act on the forward as a whole rather than on one caller, and banning an abuser would ban every external user with them. Whether that is fixed at the network end (preserving the real source) or worked around here is an open question with the administrator; the bullets below still describe the address-keyed behaviour as designed, which is what you get on a deployment without a NATing forward in front of it. The operator's own contribution path no longer needs the VPN either: the owner SRT route was moved off the Tailscale-only bind the same day, for the same reason. If you are running your own instance, none of this applies: open the ports on your own host and the endpoint is reachable immediately.

## How it behaves

- **First come, first served, one publisher at a time.** A second concurrent push is rejected outright (OBS shows "Failed to connect"), not queued. There is deliberately no reservation or key system; contention shows up in the telemetry history instead.
- While a guest publishes, the demo loop pauses and the public player page serves the guest's stream: watching your own test is just the normal player URL, shareable as-is.
- **Reconnect window:** if the publisher disconnects, the slot is held for `GUEST_GRACE_S` (default 120 s). Reconnect within it and the session continues; otherwise the session ends and the demo loop returns to its normal on-demand behaviour.
- **Session cap:** an actively publishing guest may run `GUEST_MAX_S` (default 3 h, long enough for a rehearsal or a real event). The cap is enforced within one liveness-ping interval (~10 s), so it is soft by up to that much. Reconnecting does not reset the clock.
- **Resource guard (`GUEST_MAX_TEMP_C`, `GUEST_MAX_MBPS`, both off by default):** nothing else stops a guest pushing 60 Mbps at your hardware. Temperature is the one to trust, because it is measured on the device and therefore sees room heat, dust and thermal soak; bitrate is a coarse pre-filter for the obvious abuser. The distinction is not theoretical: on the 2012 Mac Mini deployment a synthetic 42 Mbps ladder peaked at 87 C while a real 45.5 Mbps session ran 87-96 C with zero viewers, a ~9 C gap no bitrate threshold could have predicted. Set both from a measurement of YOUR host rather than copying anyone's numbers; the full reference ladder (4K30, video passed through, and the encoder never dropping below realtime, so a thermal ceiling rather than a capability one) sits beside the knobs in [.env.example](../.env.example). Neither limit fires until it holds for `GUEST_LIMIT_STRIKES` consecutive pings, and one good sample forgives the strikes.
- **Cooldown after a forced end:** when a session is ended by the cap or by the dashboard's End session button (not by a natural stop), guest publishes are refused for `GUEST_COOLDOWN_S` (default 300 s; OBS's default auto-reconnect keeps retrying for roughly 3 hours with exponential backoff, so the cooldown spaces out reclaim attempts rather than outlasting them). Without it, an auto-reconnecting encoder would re-claim the freed slot in seconds, turning the cap into a duty cycle and the kill button into a two-second blip.
- The `:8090` dashboard shows the session (name, source address, time left) and has an **End session** button; the public player page shows only endpoint state, sanitised name and time left.
- **Session log:** anonymised statistics (timestamp, name, country, duration, end reason) are kept indefinitely; the source IP is truncated to its network prefix (a.b.c.x, v6 /48) after `GUEST_RETENTION_DAYS` (default 30), keeping repeat-network patterns visible without keeping the address. Country comes from a local DB-IP lookup; guest IPs are never sent to an online service.
- **Privacy notice:** while a session is active the player shows a collapsible bilingual notice (EN default, PL switch) stating the cap, logging, retention, reporting and ban rules, with the numbers interpolated from the running config. A built-in generic text ships in the page, so the notice works with no brand.json; a deployment's `brand.json guestDisclaimer` replaces the wording wholesale.
- **Reporting:** the player page shows a report button while a guest session is active. Reports are rate-limited (per reporter IP at nginx and in the arbiter) and alert the operator; reporter IPs live under the same `GUEST_RETENTION_DAYS` redaction as everything else.
- **Bans:** the dashboard's End + ban button blocks the session's source IP for `GUEST_BAN_DAYS` (default 30, clamped to the retention window: ban expiry and IP redaction are deliberately the same event). Bans can be lifted early from the dashboard. This is a SPEED BUMP, not enforcement: dynamic IPs, CGNAT and VPNs all defeat an IP ban. It exists to make casual misuse inconvenient, nothing more.
- **Stalled transcodes:** a guest pushing the wrong audio layout (plain stereo/mono is the classic mistake) produces no playable output; the session is ended automatically after ~45 s and the reason is shown on the player page, with no cooldown so a corrected retry works immediately.
- **Fail-closed:** if telemetry is down, guest publishes are rejected while the token-authed `owner` application and the demo loop keep working.
- **Owner preemption:** a successful stream-key push to `/owner` ends any in-progress guest session automatically (the owner's `/auth` success signals the arbiter, which drops the guest the same way the dashboard's End session button does, within one liveness-ping interval). An owner broadcast can therefore start without first clearing the guest slot by hand.
- **SRT as well as RTMP:** the same arbiter also accepts native OBS multitrack over SRT via the `srt-gateway` service (`srt://<host>:8890?streamid=<name>&latency=2000000&pkt_size=1128`); slot, cap, cooldown, grace, bans and kill are shared across both transports, so the rules above apply identically however a guest connects. See [docs/obs-macos.md](obs-macos.md) or [docs/obs-windows.md](obs-windows.md) for the OBS settings.

**Network prerequisite:** the RTMP guest endpoint is exactly as public as TCP port 1935; the SRT endpoint (if enabled) is exactly as public as UDP 8890. If your host sits behind a firewall or NAT, the one thing to arrange is inbound access to whichever port(s) you use; everything else ships in this compose file.

## The owner SRT route (`SRT_MODE=owner`)

The same `srt-gateway` code runs in a second mode, for the operator's own contribution rather than a guest's. It is not wired up in the shipped `docker-compose.yml` (which sets no `SRT_MODE`, so `guest` is the default); enabling it means adding a second gateway instance in `docker-compose.override.yml`, on its own host port.

**`scripts/setup.sh` sets this up for you** and is the recommended way in: it generates `RTMP_OWNER_KEY` and `SRT_OWNER_PASSPHRASE` into `.env`, writes a minimal `docker-compose.override.yml` containing just this service, and prints the OBS URL with your real passphrase in it. (Do not instead copy `docker-compose.override.yml.example` wholesale onto a fresh clone: it also mounts `./custom/brand.json`, which does not exist there. Take the `srt-gateway-owner` block from it if you are assembling an override by hand.) This is the route to take if you want to push your own 16-channel stream from stock OBS and never enable the keyless guest endpoint: `GUEST_ENABLED` can stay `0` throughout. The OBS settings are identical to the recipe in [obs-windows.md](obs-windows.md) or [obs-macos.md](obs-macos.md) section 5 apart from the URL, which becomes `srt://<box-address>:8891?streamid=owner&passphrase=<SRT_OWNER_PASSPHRASE>&latency=2000000&pkt_size=1128`.

What it changes: the gateway accepts one multitrack SRT caller and, by default (`SRT_DIRECT=1`), remuxes it straight into earshot's direct-DASH listener, which does the join and the Opus encode. With `SRT_DIRECT=0` it instead joins the tracks itself to a named layout (4x4 to 16 channels, or a single 4-channel track passed through) and republishes into the token-authed `/owner` application with `RTMP_OWNER_KEY` instead of into `/guest`. Either way it bypasses the guest arbiter - no slot arbitration, no 3 h cap, no cooldown, no ban list - so it gives the operator an SRT sender path with the same standing as an RTMP push of the owner stream key. Both paths share `RTMP_OWNER_KEY` on purpose: it is the one credential that actually needs to resist a real attacker (unlike `LOOP_SOURCE_KEY`, which only ever gates the demo loop's docker-internal publish).

**The passphrase is the gate, and the bind is your exposure dial.** The mode does no per-caller authentication beyond the passphrase: any handshake that completes is republished with the real owner key. `setup.sh` binds it to all interfaces, matching the stack's other two contribution ports, because a contribution endpoint you can only reach from the box itself is the rare case. That is defensible because SRT uses the passphrase as the connection's AES key, so libsrt refuses the handshake before anything is parsed. Narrow the bind in the `srt-gateway-owner` ports line to `127.0.0.1` (this machine only) or a VPN address (off the internet entirely) if you want less exposure; either OBS guide's "Pushing from another machine" section covers the trade-offs.

**`SRT_OWNER_PASSPHRASE` is mandatory here, not optional.** It is the only caller gate, so the gateway refuses to start without it (`FATAL: owner mode requires SRT_OWNER_PASSPHRASE`), and equally without `RTMP_OWNER_KEY`. Those two guards are the whole access-control story for this mode.

**`SRT_OWNER_MAX_S` (default 86400, 24 h) ends a session on purpose before something else ends it by accident.** Because owner sessions skip the arbiter's cap, the first hard limit they would otherwise meet is the MPEG-TS presentation-timestamp wrap: 33 bits at 90 kHz is about 26.5 h, and how the chain behaves at that rollover has not been verified (a bare remux on the direct route, demux-join-FLV on the legacy one). A planned daily reconnect beats an unpredicted overflow. The ceiling is checked on the gateway's idle janitor tick, and the teardown drops the caller without memoizing it, so an immediate reconnect starts fresh with a fresh timestamp epoch. Set `SRT_OWNER_MAX_S=0` to disable the ceiling if you would rather find out what happens at 26.5 h. Guest mode never reads this value: telemetry's 3 h cap fires long first.
